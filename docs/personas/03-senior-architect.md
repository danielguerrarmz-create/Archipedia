# Persona 03 — Lena Vasquez, Senior Architect (10+ years)

> *"I can defend a design move in a review if I can trace its lineage. I can't
> defend it if the tool that found the precedent might have made it up — or can't
> tell me who built it."*

## Snapshot

- **Age range:** 36–44
- **Role:** Senior Architect / Associate / Design Director at a 60–120-person firm.
  Leads design across two or three concurrent projects.
- **Years in practice:** 10–15. Licensed. Has shaped buildings end-to-end and now
  shapes *other people's* buildings through direction and review.
- **Authority:** High (design). Sets the design language for her projects, runs
  internal pin-ups and design reviews, mentors intermediates and interns, presents
  to clients alongside the Principal.
- **What she does all day:** design crits, sketch overlays, steering precedent
  research her team does *for* her, building the design narrative for client
  presentations, defending moves to skeptical clients and planning boards.
- **Tools:** Rhino (fluent), Revit (reviews, not modeling), Enscape/V-Ray for
  presentation imagery, Illustrator/InDesign/Keynote for narrative decks, Miro for
  team boards, Morpholio/iPad for sketch overlays, and a deep personal precedent
  archive (Are.na, physical monographs, conference talks).

## Goals

1. **Establish and communicate a defensible design language** — precedent isn't
   decoration, it's the *argument* for why the building is the way it is.
2. **Build a traceable precedent lineage** she can stand behind in a review or in
   front of a client ("this references X, which solved Y the same way").
3. **Direct her team's research efficiently** — she sets the thread, they pull it.
4. **Present beautifully and credibly** — the precedent board *is* the slide.
5. **Mentor taste** — show juniors what rigorous precedent selection looks like.

## Context & a day-in-the-life

Thursday client presentation for a cultural building. Lena has the concept; she
needs the precedent narrative to make it land — three or four built projects that
establish the lineage of her massing and material strategy, each credited and
contextualized, arranged so the *relationship* between them reads at a glance. She
hands Dev and Maya the thread ("courtyard civic, heavy base / light roof, warm
climate"), reviews what they bring, and curates the final set herself. In the
review she'll be asked, pointedly, *"why this, why now, what's the precedent"* — and
"I saw it on Pinterest" is not an answer. She needs provenance and a coherent visual
argument.

## How she'd use Archipedia

- Use the **Compare** and **Compose** ideas directly: put two precedents side by
  side to surface the formal relationship, then pull a curated set onto the canvas
  and **draw the connections** between them — the lineage made explicit and visible.
- Tune fusion weights deliberately — raise **Spatial** when the *organizational*
  logic is the argument, **Regional** for a climate-driven narrative — and expect
  the results to reflect that intent.
- Curate down to a tight, fully-cited set and export a board that doubles as a
  presentation slide — "a clean, credible sheet, not a mood board."
- Set up a starter graph and hand it to her team to extend, then review their work.

## Pain points & what would make her churn

- **Indefensible provenance.** If she can't trace a precedent to architect / project
  / year / location, it's useless for a review — and *worse*, embarrassing if a
  client or board member knows the real attribution and she got it wrong.
- **Any hint of fabrication.** If the system can return a plausible building that
  isn't real, her professional credibility is on the line every time she presents.
  This is disqualifying, not a nuisance.
- **A board that looks like a mood board.** If the export reads as a Pinterest grid,
  it undercuts the rigor she's trying to project. The product *promises* "not a mood
  board" — it has to deliver that.
- **A canvas that's clever but doesn't make the *argument* visible.** If connecting
  nodes is fiddly or the relationships don't read, the core value (lineage made
  legible) is lost.
- **A tool she can't safely standardize across her team** — inconsistent behavior
  or trust issues mean she won't make it the team's method.

## What would delight her

- Side-by-side compare that surfaces a relationship she *felt* but couldn't
  articulate — and lets her name it on the canvas.
- A precedent board where the *connections* carry meaning and export as a credible,
  credited narrative slide.
- Deliberate weight control that visibly reshapes results toward her intent
  (spatial vs. regional vs. visual), so the tool feels like an instrument, not a toy.
- A clean handoff to her team: she seeds the graph, they extend it, she curates.

## First-run review

**First 60 seconds.** She reads the splash as a *design statement*, and it mostly
works — "sorted by visual proximity, not keywords" and "a sheet that holds up in
review" are speaking her language. The Compare and Compose narrative sections
preview exactly the lineage-making she does manually. She's intrigued but reserving
judgment until she sees provenance and the export.

**Where she gets confused / hits an error.** She enters the canvas and immediately
feels the **two-product seam**: the light, refined "concrete" marketing pages give
way to a dark "studio" canvas with no narration — it reads as a different app bolted
on. She wires up a Compare/Compose graph, presses **RUN**, and… nothing visibly
resolves (failure swallowed). Then, exploring, she notices the canvas search can
surface results while the Classic page says the index is down — and the moment she
suspects results *might* be fabricated, her trust collapses. For Lena this isn't a
bug, it's a credibility threat: she cannot put a possibly-invented precedent in
front of a client. She also wants the documented default weights (Visual 100%) to
match what the canvas actually does (33/33/34) — the inconsistency reads as
imprecision in a tool she's evaluating for rigor.

**What feels weak/static.** Connections between precedent nodes exist but don't yet
*carry an argument* — she wants to label a relationship ("shared courtyard logic")
and have that survive into the export. Canvas node metadata is thinner than the
Classic cards, so the lineage isn't fully citable where she'd build it.

**Fixes she'd ask for (prioritized):**
1. **Provenance is non-negotiable and must be uniform.** Architect · project ·
   location · year on every node, card, and export — or "unrecorded," never blank,
   never invented. (→ F4)
2. **Eliminate any path to fabricated results.** The canvas must fail as honestly as
   the Classic page. Trust is the product. (→ F1, F3)
3. **Make the connection mean something.** Let me label edges between precedents and
   carry those labels + credits into the exported board as a *narrative*. (→ F9/export)
4. **Narrate the handoff into the canvas** and reconcile the two visual languages so
   it reads as one rigorous product, not a marketing site plus a tool. (→ F6)
5. **Make weights an honest instrument** — reconcile documented vs. actual defaults
   and show, even briefly, how moving Spatial/Regional reshapes the set. (→ F8)
