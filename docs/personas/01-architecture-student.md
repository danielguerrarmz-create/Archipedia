# Persona 01 — Maya Okonkwo, Architecture Student (3rd year, summer intern)

> *"If it's faster than digging through Arch Daily tabs and I can actually drop a
> credit next to the image, I'm using this for everything — and so is the rest of
> studio."*

## Snapshot

- **Age range:** 20–22
- **Role:** Third-year B.Arch student; landed a competitive summer internship at a
  ~30-person award-winning urban firm.
- **Years in practice:** <1 (intern). Effectively an *extension* of the Principal
  and a Mid-level Designer — she executes their direction.
- **Authority:** Low. High energy, fast with software, building precedent literacy.
- **What she actually does all day:** precedent hunting for the boss and her own
  studio project, diagrams, board/sheet assembly, render touch-ups, "find me five
  more like this by EOD" requests.
- **Tools:** Rhino (+ Grasshopper, learning), some Revit, Enscape for quick views,
  Illustrator/InDesign for boards, Photoshop for render fixes, Figma occasionally,
  and *constantly* Pinterest, Arch Daily, Dezeen, Divisare, plus a chaotic mess of
  browser tabs and a personal Are.na. Miro for pinning things with classmates.

## Goals

1. **Find strong, relevant precedents fast** — for the boss's project *and* her own
   studio work — without 30 open tabs.
2. **Build a precedent/mood board that looks credible**, not like a Pinterest dump,
   because it gets pinned up in review next to the Principal's.
3. **Keep the citation attached** — architect, project, year — because professors
   and the Principal both call out uncredited images.
4. **Look competent and fast** in front of people with authority over her.
5. **Learn** what "good precedent" even looks like — she's still forming taste.

## Context & a day-in-the-life

9:30am — Mid-level designer drops a Slack: *"For the riverfront civic thing, find
me precedents — board-formed concrete, big roof overhangs, tropical-ish. Like 8–10,
by lunch."* Maya opens five tabs: Arch Daily search, two Pinterest boards, Divisare,
and a Google Images rabbit hole. By 11 she has 40 screenshots in a Figma frame, half
with no idea who designed them. She spends 45 minutes back-tracing credits. At
lunch she sends a contact sheet; the designer likes 3, says "more like *that* one,"
and she starts over. In the afternoon she switches hats to her own studio project
(a library) and does the same hunt for herself. She lives in the gap between "I can
see exactly what they want" and "I have no fast way to get it credited."

## How she'd use Archipedia

- Type the boss's brief almost verbatim into the splash search:
  `civic concrete · brise-soleil · tropical`.
- Skim ranked results, "Search like this" off the one the designer liked to chase
  the *visual* thread keyword search can't follow.
- Drag the keepers onto the canvas, arrange a quick precedent board, export a PDF
  contact sheet to drop in Slack — *with credits already on it.*
- Use it for her own studio project the same way, after hours.
- If it's genuinely faster and the boards look sharp, she becomes the firm's
  unofficial evangelist — interns spread tools.

## Pain points & what would make her churn

- **Anything slower than her tab-soup.** If search is laggy, empty, or makes her
  learn a node graph before she gets *one* image, she's back on Pinterest in 90
  seconds. The canvas's 12 node types + operators look like homework.
- **Missing or wrong credits.** If the architect/year isn't on the image, the tool
  hasn't solved her actual pain — it's just a prettier image search.
- **A dead-feeling first run.** If she searches and gets a spinner that never
  resolves, or fake-looking results, she assumes it's broken or fake and leaves.
- **Ugly exports.** If the PDF looks worse than what she can do in InDesign, she
  won't use it for anything that goes on a wall.

## What would delight her

- Splash search → real, beautiful, *credited* results in one gesture (the rotating
  example queries already nudge her toward good prompts — nice).
- "Search like this" off an image to ride the visual thread — the thing Pinterest
  fakes and keywords can't do.
- One-click "send selected to board → export sheet" that looks better than her
  InDesign default, credits baked in.
- It feels *cooler* than Pinterest — the dark canvas, the motion. Cool matters to a
  3rd-year; it's social currency in studio.

## Success metric

Time-to-credited-board: from brief → a sharp, fully-cited 8-image sheet in under
10 minutes (vs. her current ~90). Secondary: she sends a teammate the link unprompted.

## First-run review

**First 60 seconds.** The splash lands hard — "Press your idea against everything
ever built," the dark hero, the annotated subject diagram, rotating placeholder
queries. She immediately gets it and types `civic concrete brise-soleil tropical`.
She likes that there's a real building credited in the hero (architect · country ·
INDEXED) — *that's the Pinterest delta she's been missing.*

**Where she gets confused / hits an error.** She hits Search and lands on the
Classic results page… which (backend off) shows an honest "Couldn't reach the index
— check your connection" panel. Mildly annoying but she *trusts* it; she'll come
back. Then she finds the **Canvas** and runs a search there — and gets results that
look real but are actually mock data, *or* she presses the big blue **RUN** button
and **nothing visibly happens** (failure is swallowed to the console). Now she's
confused: "Did it work? Is this real? Why does one page say it's down and the other
shows stuff?" That inconsistency is exactly the kind of thing that makes a student
quietly distrust a tool. The canvas itself — rail, `/` command palette, AND/OR/NOT,
fusion weights — reads as "this is for someone more senior than me."

**What feels weak/static.** Empty canvas is sparse — a NodeField sketch and three
templates; she's not sure which template gets her to "8 credited images" fastest.
The compact results grid on the canvas shows thinner credits than the hero card, so
the citation promise wobbles right where she'd act on it.

**Fixes she'd ask for (prioritized):**
1. **"Just give me results, don't make me learn a graph."** Land me on the canvas
   with my search already run and results showing — let me drag, then *optionally*
   discover the node stuff. (→ F5)
2. **Make RUN obviously do something.** Spinner, then either results or a clear
   "couldn't reach the index, try again." Right now I can't tell if I broke it. (→ F2)
3. **Put the architect + year on *every* image**, including on the canvas and in
   the board grid — that's the whole reason I'd switch from Pinterest. (→ F4)
4. **Be consistent about whether you're up.** One page errors honestly, another
   shows stuff — pick one so I know whether to trust it. (→ F1, F3)
5. **Make the export sheet a one-click hero** off my selected images, credits baked
   in, prettier than my InDesign default.
